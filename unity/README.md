# ZERO // RISE – Unity Windows prototype

This is a separate, playable district prototype. The existing browser game in `dist/` is preserved. Its economy, missions, survival, tuning and save files are **not yet ported**. Unity now has its own district traffic system.

## Open and play

Open `unity/ZeroRise` in Unity Hub using **Unity 6000.6.5f1**, the version installed on this machine. HDRP **17.6.0** is pinned to that editor. Include Windows Build Support when installing the editor. On first import the editor script generates `Assets/ZeroRise/Scenes/Westbruch.unity`, its prefabs, shared meshes, terrain and materials. A generated scene is also saved in the project after successful local validation.

Open that scene and press Play. WASD walks at 1.45 m/s, Alt jogs at 3.2 m/s, Shift sprints at 5.8 m/s, Space jumps, mouse orbits, Escape releases the mouse. Hold the right mouse button to align the standing character with the camera. Approach the driver's side of the green compact and press E. The character aligns, reaches for the handle, opens the hinged door, ducks into the seat and closes the door. The driver remains visible with both hands following the rotating wheel through generic-rig arm IK. WASD drives/steers; Space brakes. Stop before pressing E to exit. Blocked doors are rejected; the passenger side is used if the driver's exit is obstructed. Movement and vehicle controls are locked during boarding, while camera orbit remains available. Disabling the vehicle safely restores the character controller.

The player uses the browser hero's recognizable streetwear with refined geometry, continuous skinned sleeves/jeans, facial details, fabric normals, shoe laces and backpack stitching. The 20-bone generic rig has 15 baked clips for locomotion, idle actions, jumping/landing and visible vehicle interactions. Walking, jogging and sprinting share a continuous step phase driven by actual distance travelled, using the Animator's motion-time parameter. Speed changes retain that phase; acceleration/stopping blend directly with a separate idle state instead of taking over the legs with stationary-foot anticipation clips. Slow movement slows the walk cycle; idle contact curves cannot contaminate moving gait blends. Shorter stance travel keeps legs within reach. Bounded pelvis motion replaces the previous swing-foot-driven height correction; chest counter-motion, heel/toe roll, blinking and backpack response remain. Animated contact curves drive stationary world foot anchors and local terrain support. Planted feet retain their facing; sharp turns smoothly release a contact before the next step. An unreachable retained contact triggers a small smooth settling step that returns continuously to the current authored pose; newly airborne feet are not locked and sole penetration is corrected. Translation and character facing share one heading, with braking during sharp direction changes to avoid sideways sliding. A visual ground offset compensates for controller contact skin without changing the capsule. Animation follows actual movement, including stopping against walls; nearby ground support prevents tiny controller contact gaps from repeatedly restarting landing. Movement accelerates/decelerates smoothly, with 120 ms jump buffering/ground grace. Capsule dimensions, gravity, jump impulse and vehicle physics are preserved. Small controller moves are retained at high frame rates. The character and car remain stylized prototype art.

Idle has a relaxed stance with slight knee flexion, subtle breathing and a smaller occasional glance after 18 seconds. Solver tolerances cannot retrigger settling steps while standing. On uneven ground a smoothly adjusted stationary stance keeps the feet supported; this adjustment remains active through Idle/IdleLook blends and does not use swing feet. Turn clips explicitly release the lifted foot. The player Animator evaluates offscreen so secondary pose offsets receive a fresh authored pose each frame.

The `ZERO RISE` editor menu can regenerate the district, validate physical placements and build `Builds/Windows/ZeroRise.exe`. Regeneration replaces the generated scene: save hand-authored work in a separate scene before using it. Editable building prefabs are under `Assets/ZeroRise/Generated/Prefabs`.

## District

264 × 228 metre scene inspired by the supplied references, with a connected street ring, courtyards, 20 residential/commercial buildings, playground, garage row, service yard, PFAND & SPAR, bus shelter, canal and railway bridge. Sizes and positions are deliberately adapted rather than copied from the reference map. Doors are exterior detail; building interiors are not implemented. Temporary construction fencing marks the edge of the playable prototype and prevents falling off the Terrain.

True sculptable Terrain now blends four ground layers: worn soil/grit, sparse grass, mud and playground sand. The ground has subtle urban relief, flattened circulation/foundations, a recessed canal and an extended non-playable terrain backdrop. Physically based HDRP materials and sky, volumetric clouds, sunlight, fog, ACES tonemapping, temporal antialiasing and ambient occlusion provide the rendering foundation. Shared meshes/materials and baked editable prefabs avoid runtime geometry generation. Static details are combined into spatial render chunks while their authoring sources and colliders remain in the scene. There are 37 trees using four newly authored broadleaf silhouettes with connected curved branches, a rounded root collar and thousands of individual textured leaves. Each shares two cached geometry LODs, separate trunk collision and restrained crown wind. Four reusable tree prefabs and shared bark/leaf materials are saved under Generated/. Folded, curved mesh blades provide grass; curved stalks and individually folded leaves provide brambles/nettles. One shared GPU animation adds wind and smooth player/car contact bending with rooted bases and recovery. Roads, house approaches and parking are excluded. No ray tracing is required. Hardware performance must be assessed in the actual Windows build.

The ground asset pass uses a 1025-square native heightmap with sculpted courtyard mounds, shallow drainage hollows and smaller soil folds. All 46 connected walks are included in the flattening mask before terrain generation. Vehicle/equipment bearing pads and the playground remain supported. Four height-blended PBR layers use coherent stochastic sampling and broad tonal variation; grassy surfaces have a finer real-world scale. Twelve thousand embedded gravel, rubble and soil pieces add actual silhouettes and shadows, baked into shared spatial meshes with no individual colliders or runtime mesh generation. `Assets/ZeroRise/Generated/Prefabs/WestbruchGroundAsset.prefab` contains the editable Terrain and baked relief; it shares the scene's TerrainData and materials, so edits to those shared assets affect both.

Ground painting and vegetation now share an authoring field for wear, sheltered edges, tree cover and shallow damp pockets. The large evenly scattered nettles have been replaced by six smaller plant families with three cached variants each: short dry grass, sparse sedge/seed heads, ground rosettes, branching weeds, open woody shrubs and occasional pale wildflowers. Artist-space colors are converted to linear vertex colors and multiplied by one shared neutral material so foliage fits the muted soil palette. The saved district contains 39,109 clumps in uneven colonies, with bare compacted gravel between them. Grass source height is 13–25 cm; taller shrubs are uncommon. Vegetation is placed after the yard props using its final transformed bounds, so bins, planters, trees, paths and entrances are respected. Roots follow the existing terrain, and the shared wind/contact animation remains active. The 64 foliage chunks contain approximately 5.8 million triangles over the whole district; actual hardware performance still needs profiling.

The nature asset pass adds a darker, finer earth surface, packed leaf albedo atlas, bark detail and derived normal/mask maps imported at 2048 square. Generated source images are 1254 square; resampling does not add photographic detail. Exact prompts and processing notes are in `Assets/ZeroRise/Art/Nature2K/SOURCE.md`. Ground-following leaf drifts, fallen twigs and small rubble collect under actual crowns and along sheltered edges. Dense low shrub groups and a supported shared seating area give the southern courtyard a clear use, reached by a worn ground path connected to the existing paving. Litter is baked into shared spatial meshes without extra collision or per-frame geometry work. Fifty-one low courtyard shrubs are distributed across all four parts of the district, including the eastern and outer yards, with two shared geometry LODs. The low courtyard shrubs are fully passable for the player and camera. Their separate shared shader bends stems and foliage smoothly around rooted bases in response to swept player/car contact, then relaxes after passing. They retain per-object roots rather than entering static transform batching. Large tree trunks remain solid. Exposed root pegs have been removed, and tree forks use slimmer curved, tapering limbs with subtle age variation.

Roads now share continuous world-space asphalt with blended, irregular texture sampling, aligned normal/roughness detail and broad color variation. Individual branched crack ribbons and irregular repair polygons provide unique wear instead of repeating rectangular patches. All original circulation colliders remain. Technical notes and the HDRP-derived shader license are in `Assets/ZeroRise/Art/URBAN-RENDERING.md`.

The visual update adds facade shutters/curtains, varied roof profiles, shop displays, bicycles, washing lines, raised beds, refuse/salvage corners, clearer road details, a glass bus shelter and supported railway continuations. Twenty-four simplified blocks beyond the construction boundary provide visual skyline context; they are not additional migrated playable districts. New materials and their original generation prompts/provenance are in `Assets/ZeroRise/Art/SurfacesV3/`.

## Vehicles and district traffic

Physical vehicle contact now uses two closed convex shapes: a chamfered low body and a sloped cabin/roof. The disabled root box is only a conservative traffic verification envelope; it no longer blocks empty air above the bonnet or luggage deck. Player actor sweeps use the actual separation normal for zero-distance contacts so existing contact does not prevent moving away or sliding along a vehicle. `VehicleCornerQA.Run` checks all four NPC families and the player car, including moving corners, reversal and side sliding.

Cars also have inset inner shells, full formed floor pans, wheel tubs, firewall, deck undersides, headlining, parcel shelves and moving door armrests. `VehicleInteriorQA.Run` casts from inside against the saved visible surfaces, complementing the exterior coverage check. `VehicleBodyShowcase.Capture` now writes twenty exterior and fifteen interior native HDRP views; `interior-*` views use a short inspection-camera near clip, without changing the gameplay camera.

Four unbranded, locally generated vehicle families replace the simple boxes: hatchback, sedan, estate and delivery van, with six shared paint finishes. Shaped wheel arches, bevelled panels, transparent glazing, mirrors, handles, wipers, cabin seats, numberplates, lamp clusters, round tyres and open alloy spokes add visible detail. The estate has roof rails and the van a taller cargo body. Parked cars and the driveable compact use the same asset family; the player's existing wheel physics, seat/steering anchors and animated door interaction remain. The compact roof includes matching collision and clearance for the seated player's hair. Vehicle meshes are cached and combined by material while preserving animated wheels, doors and steering. No downloaded vehicle models, brands or additional dynamic lights are used.

Twelve visible NPC drivers circulate through nine intersections and 24 directed right-hand lanes on the district's existing roads. Their route graph excludes U-turns and chooses between straight movement and legal turns. Arc-length sampled curves provide continuous turning, with animated wheel steering, wheel rotation, brake lamps and turn signals. Shared junction reservations, main-road priority, right-hand yielding, clearance of the exit lane and aged-wait fairness manage conflicting approaches. Forward physics probes include vehicles, the real player capsule and solid obstacles; vehicles brake, queue and resume when the lane clears. One snapshot supplies every vehicle's speed decision before positions are committed. These are kinematic traffic agents; overtaking, traffic-light control and a complete city traffic simulation are outside this district implementation. The player's camera and boarding/exit checks include the NPC collision layer.

`WestbruchVehicleArt.RebuildAndCapture` updates only vehicles and traffic in the saved scene. `WestbruchTrafficQA.Run` checks all legal turn footprints and simulates two timestep runs, then renders six actual HDRP vehicle/junction views. `WestbruchTrafficPlayQA.Run` verifies real FixedUpdate/Rigidbody motion, pairwise collision clearance, stopping for a physical obstacle and the player, and resumption. `WestbruchTrafficPlayQA.CaptureOccupant` checks animated entry/exit and actual skinned-mesh roof clearance, and captures the seated player. Reports and native views are under `QA/traffic-*`, `QA/vehicles-*` and `QA/vehicle-*`.

## Residents and vehicle refinement

The vehicle shoulder meshes now use explicit outward winding on both sides; the previous mirrored upper fender was backface culled and appeared as a long opening below the hood/rear deck. Door openings follow the actual moving panel edges, with recessed metal jambs. Fixed front pillars and the van's sealed front quarter windows close the space beside the moving doors. `VehicleBodyQA.Run` checks the saved, visible combined body and cabin meshes from both sides and ends, including all four traffic families, parked cars and the player car. `VehicleBodyShowcase.Capture` renders twenty native front/rear views from both sides for inspection.

Stationary residents own their activity position instead of allowing native avoidance to displace a stopped agent. Their actual-position obstacles make passing residents go around them. Sitting and standing advance according to collision-safe achieved movement, and navigation resumes at the reached position. Player movement sweeps against resident/traffic bodies and preserves tangential sliding. `ResidentContactQA.Run` exercises continuous sprint contact rather than only placing a stationary obstacle ahead of a walking agent.

Animated vehicle interactions continuously check the player's capsule and the door's changing sweep against other actors. An arriving resident pauses the pose and movement until the path clears. Press E again to cancel; an eight-second continuous blockage also triggers recovery. An interrupted approach returns control at its clear standing position, while a blocked exit can ease back to the seat and restore driving controls. Recovery checks the whole movement path, and doors close only when their sweep is clear. Normal restoration requires the reached, grounded, unoccupied endpoint; disabling a vehicle can instead search nearby clear ground if the original position is occupied. `VehicleInteractionContactQA.Run` tests arriving actors, retained blockers and interrupted cleanup.

The district also has eighteen outdoor residents with varied streetwear, skin/hair tones and the existing skinned animation rig. They choose reachable destinations at houses, the shop, workshop, playground surroundings, bus stop and benches instead of random world coordinates. Distance, recent visits, rest after walking and an available conversation partner influence their choices. Seats and activity slots have exclusive owners; sitting and standing blend continuously, with phone, conversation, waiting, looking and stretching poses. Several activities are already occupied at startup. Interiors and a full simulated household/economy are separate systems.

The saved resident NavMesh is built from actual terrain and solid colliders, including fences, trees, parked vehicles and furniture. Soil travel costs more than paved routes. Roads are excluded from free walking; eighteen bidirectional links provide designated marked crossings. Native high-quality local avoidance handles walking encounters, with dynamic obstacles for the player and driveable car. Actual seated/crossing positions also have avoidance footprints. Manual transitions use capsule sweeps, and blocked routes are retried or replaced with another meaningful destination. Crossing ownership and stopping-distance checks coordinate pedestrians with NPC traffic before anyone enters the road. Unavailable seats, incomplete paths and blocked landings cannot silently become random targets.

`ZERO RISE/Fussgaenger und Aktivitaeten aktualisieren` adds/updates residents in the existing saved district. `WestbruchPedestrianBuilder.RebuildAll` updates the refined cars and residents together; it does not rebuild the terrain, buildings or flora. `WestbruchPedestrianQA.Run` validates destination connectivity and runs actual resident/traffic navigation, activities, mutual clearance and a player-obstacle case. Native resident and vehicle detail views are in `QA/resident-vehicle-*.png`. Navigation uses Unity's built-in AI module and [NavMeshAgent](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/ScriptReference/AI.NavMeshAgent.html).

The vehicle follow-up closes the engine shoulder, luggage deck and front/rear chamfer contours. Lower-profile inset hoods, door protection/shut lines, rear seats or a van cargo floor/bulkhead, center console, gear lever, dashboard vents, inset lamps, reversing lenses, brake discs and wheel bolts complete previously sparse areas. Wagon rails have mounting feet. The former primitive NPC driver shapes are replaced with the existing detailed generic rig, sampled seated pose, face, fingers, varied materials and runtime steering-hand IK. All vehicle and resident art remains locally generated/reused project art; no external models were downloaded.
